The Transit Bottleneck

Why Tap-and-Scan Ticketing Costs Cities Millions Every Year

Public transport operators face a painful trade-off: enforce strict front-door validation and slow down every route, or allow all-door boarding and lose millions in unvalidated rides and missing transfer data.

1. The “Forgotten Transfer” Leak

In Rīga, 90-minute tickets and passes require re-registration every time a rider switches vehicles. With winter gloves, bags, or crowded doors, paying commuters skip tapping—turning honest riders into technical fare evaders and distorting route-level subsidy allocation.

4–9% unrecorded boardings on multi-leg trips

2. Door Queues Cause Bus Bunching

Scanning QR codes on dim phone screens or hunting for plastic cards adds 2.5–4 seconds per passenger at major stops like Centrālā stacija. Multiply that across 18 stops and a single delayed bus triggers platoon bunching across the corridor.

+14–22% peak-hour stop dwell overhead

3. Blind Cabins & Driver Friction

Drivers shouldn’t have to act as ticket police—yet today neither drivers, dispatchers, nor waiting passengers know how many people are actually on board, how many registered a valid ticket, or whether the rear cabin still has room.

Zero real-time compliance visibility today

Key Questions Answered

Operator, Technical & Regulatory FAQ

Does hands-free boarding work when the passenger’s phone is locked in their pocket?

Yes in production native apps: while our browser-based interactive demo simulates the BLE radio so you can test it on any device today, the production iOS and Android apps use OS-level hardware beacon wakeups (CoreLocation region monitoring on iOS and PendingIntent BLE scan filters on Android) to wake EasyRide in the background for ~3 seconds upon entering a vehicle cabin—with < 1% daily battery impact.

What happens with tourists, seniors, or riders using plastic e-talons?

100% coexistence. Existing contactless card validators and printed QR codes remain active. On the Bus Driver Cabin Console, infrared door counters (APC) measure total physical riders while subtracting both EasyRide BLE registrations and legacy validator taps—giving operators accurate compliance and crowding metrics across all passengers from day one.

Are bus drivers expected to confront unregistered passengers?

Never. EasyRide is explicitly designed for non-confrontational operations: drivers keep eyes on the road while cabin compliance data automatically triggers a polite multilingual cabin speaker chime when needed and routes mobile fare-inspection teams to the exact routes and hours where evasion actually occurs.

How is passenger GDPR privacy protected?

Beacons are transmit-only (one-way broadcast from ceiling to phone)—the bus never scans passenger MAC addresses or tracks bystanders on the street. Location and BLE checks run strictly on the rider’s handset; the operator backend receives only the valid ticket registration event (route, stop, timestamp) required for fare accounting.
